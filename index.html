Chapter 1

Europe: where the game grew up

Rain, scarves, standing walls of noise and rivalries older than most of our grandparents.

🏴 The rulebook years

England's Football Association formed in 1863 and helped turn many local versions of the game into one set of rules. Sheffield FC, founded in 1857, is recognised as the world's oldest football club still going.

💛 The Yellow Wall

Borussia Dortmund's Südtribüne is one of Europe's biggest standing terraces, with room for around 25,000 fans on one side of the ground. Germany still protects fan ownership through its famous 50+1 rule.

🎵 You'll Never Walk Alone

Liverpool fans sing it before every home game at Anfield, hands raised and scarves stretched high. The song began in a musical and became a football hymn.

🔴🔵 Més que un club

Barcelona's motto means "more than a club". El Clásico against Real Madrid is as much about identity, history and pride as it is about points.

🦁 Only locals (mostly)

Athletic Club in Bilbao is famous for its long tradition of building a squad around players from the Basque region, and it has never been relegated from La Liga.

🍀 Derby days

Celtic v Rangers (the Old Firm) splits Glasgow. In Milan, AC and Inter even share the same stadium, San Siro, for the Derby della Madonnina.

The story fans love to tell: on Christmas 1914, soldiers on the Western Front are said to have paused the fighting and played football in no man's land. Historians debate the details, but the legend shows how deeply the game reaches people.

Chapter 2

South America: football as street poetry

Here the game starts in the dust, on the beach and in the potrero, long before any stadium.

🇧🇷 Five stars on the shirt

Brazil is the only nation to have won the World Cup five times: 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994 and 2002. Pelé won three of them and made "the beautiful game" a global phrase.

😢 The Maracanazo

In 1950 Brazil needed only a draw at home in Rio. Uruguay won 2-1 in front of a huge crowd at the Maracanã, and Brazilians still talk about that silence.

🏝️ Futsal's roots

Futsal, the fast five-a-side game, is widely traced back to Montevideo, Uruguay in 1930. It became a training ground for countless Brazilian ball artists.

🇦🇷 Boca v River

The Superclásico divides Buenos Aires. Fans say La Bombonera "doesn't tremble, it beats like a heart" when Boca's supporters jump together.

✋ One match, two Maradonas

In the 1986 World Cup quarter-final against England, Diego Maradona scored the infamous "Hand of God" and, minutes later, a solo run many call the goal of the century.

🏆 Messi's missing piece

Lionel Messi finally lifted the World Cup in Qatar in 2022 after a 3-3 final with France that Argentina won on penalties, 4-2.

Chapter 3

Africa & Asia: joy, surprises and good manners

Where underdogs rewrite the script and the fans bring a whole different kind of energy.

🕺 Roger Milla's dance

At Italia 90, Cameroon's Roger Milla scored as a veteran sub and celebrated by dancing at the corner flag. Cameroon became the first African team to reach a World Cup quarter-final.

🦁 Senegal shocks France

In the opening match of the 2002 World Cup, debutants Senegal beat the reigning champions France 1-0 and reached the quarter-finals.

🇲🇦 Morocco makes history

In 2022 Morocco became the first African team to reach a World Cup semi-final, with a defence that conceded almost nothing and a crowd that turned stadiums red.

🕊️ Drogba's plea

After Ivory Coast qualified for the 2006 World Cup, Didier Drogba and his team-mates appealed on live TV for peace in their country, one of football's most remembered moments off the pitch.

🧹 Japan's tidy fans

Japanese supporters became famous at the 2018 and 2022 World Cups for cleaning their section of the stands after matches, win or lose.

🇰🇷 Korea's red wave

When South Korea co-hosted the 2002 World Cup with Japan, the "Red Devils" filled city squares and the team reached the semi-finals, the best Asian run ever.

🇮🇩 Indonesia's early moment

In 1938 the Dutch East Indies, the team that stands as Indonesia's forerunner, became the first Asian side to play at a World Cup. Today Indonesian supporters are known for passionate, loud and loyal stadium support.

Chapter 4

Fan rituals: the stuff that makes a matchday

Before kick-off, there's a whole world of habits, songs and superstitions.

🧣 The scarf

In England, club scarves became a symbol of belonging. Fans hold them high for songs like YNWA and wear them even when the weather isn't cold.

🎨 Tifo

Ultras groups, a culture that grew strongly in Italy from the 1960s, are known for tifo: giant banners and card displays that cover whole stands with art.

🌊 The Mexican Wave

The wave swept the world after the 1986 World Cup in Mexico, when stadium crowds everywhere started to stand and sit in a rolling ripple.

🎺 Vuvuzela summer

The 2010 World Cup in South Africa made the vuvuzela horn famous. Some loved the buzz, some begged for earplugs, nobody forgot it.

🥧 Matchday food

Pies and Bovril in Britain, choripán near Argentine stadiums, grilled snacks outside grounds across Asia and Africa. Every country has a taste of football.

🏘️ Street football

Park, beach, alley or car park: in nearly every country, the first goalposts are two bags or a pair of flip-flops. That's where the love begins.

Try this: ask any football fan about their first match. You'll get a story, a smell, a song and probably a ten-minute speech.

Chapter 5

World Cup & the women's game

The biggest stage on earth, and a fight to give half the world's players theirs too.

🇺🇾 1930: the beginning

The first World Cup was held in Uruguay with just 13 teams. Uruguay won it at home, beating Argentina in the final.

❄️ Qatar 2022

The first World Cup in the Middle East and the first played in November and December, so it dodged the desert summer heat.

🌎 2026: three hosts, 48 teams

The 2026 tournament is hosted jointly by the United States, Canada and Mexico and expanded to 48 teams, the biggest World Cup ever.

👟 Dick, Kerr Ladies

On Boxing Day 1920, a women's match at Goodison Park drew a crowd reported around 53,000. In 1921 the English FA banned women's matches from league grounds, a ban that lasted until 1971.

🌐 Women's World Cup

The first official FIFA Women's World Cup took place in China in 1991, and the USA won it. Interest has grown with every tournament since.

🦁 Wembley roars

England's Lionesses won Euro 2022 at Wembley in front of 87,192 fans, a record crowd for a men's or women's European Championship match.

The beautiful thing: a game with two goals and a ball keeps finding new people. If you read this far, you're already part of it. ⚽
